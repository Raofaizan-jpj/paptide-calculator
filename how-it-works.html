How Peptide Calculations Work

Peptide calculations are built on fundamental chemical arithmetic: mass normalization, volumetric concentration, and dimensional analysis. Here is a comprehensive breakdown of every equation and step.

1. Step One: Base Unit Normalization

Before performing any mathematical division or multiplication, all inputs must be converted into common base units. Without normalization, dividing micrograms by milliliters or milligrams by liters leads to massive exponential calculation errors.

Our calculation engine normalizes all mass inputs into milligrams (mg) and all liquid volumes into milliliters (mL):

Measurement Group Entered Unit Conversion to Base Base Unit
Mass Grams (g) Multiply by 1,000 mg
Mass Milligrams (mg) Multiply by 1 (native) mg
Mass Micrograms (mcg) Divide by 1,000 (or multiply by 0.001) mg
Liquid Volume Liters (L) Multiply by 1,000 mL
Liquid Volume Milliliters (mL) Multiply by 1 (native) mL

2. Concentration Formula

Concentration represents the amount of solute mass dissolved per unit of liquid solvent volume. The fundamental formula is:

Concentration Equation:

Concentration (mg/mL) = Peptide Mass (mg) ÷ Liquid Volume (mL)

To express concentration in micrograms per milliliter (mcg/mL), multiply the result by 1,000:
Concentration (mcg/mL) = Concentration (mg/mL) × 1,000

Ex

Worked Arithmetic Example:

Suppose a vial contains 5 mg of peptide powder and is reconstituted with 2 mL of bacteriostatic water.
• Concentration = 5 mg ÷ 2 mL = 2.5 mg/mL
• In micrograms: 2.5 mg/mL × 1,000 = 2,500 mcg/mL.

3. Dose-to-Volume Formula

Once concentration is established, calculating the liquid volume needed for any desired user-entered dose requires rearranging the concentration equation:

Required Volume Equation:

Required Volume (mL) = Desired Dose (mg) ÷ Concentration (mg/mL)

If the dose is provided in micrograms (mcg), convert it to milligrams first by dividing by 1,000 before dividing by concentration in mg/mL.

Ex

Worked Arithmetic Example:

Using the previous solution of 2.5 mg/mL (2,500 mcg/mL), suppose an educational user enters a dose of 250 mcg:
• Convert dose: 250 mcg ÷ 1,000 = 0.25 mg
• Volume = 0.25 mg ÷ 2.5 mg/mL = 0.1 mL.

4. Understanding Syringe Markings & Units

Standard insulin/peptide syringes are typically calibrated in "Units" rather than decimal milliliters to make micro-volumes easier to measure accurately:

  • U-100 Syringe: Designed so that 100 Units = 1.0 mL. Therefore, 1 Unit = 0.01 mL. To calculate units: Volume in mL × 100. (e.g. 0.1 mL = 10 units).
  • 0.5 mL U-100 Syringe: Holds up to 50 Units (0.5 mL). Each unit still equals 0.01 mL.
  • 0.3 mL U-100 Syringe: Holds up to 30 Units (0.3 mL), often featuring half-unit marks for precision.
  • U-40 Syringe: Designed so that 40 Units = 1.0 mL. Therefore, 1 Unit = 0.025 mL. To calculate units: Volume in mL × 40.

5. Strict Mathematical Boundary

Medical dosing depends on clinical diagnosis, pharmaceutical product monographs, and guidance from licensed healthcare providers.
