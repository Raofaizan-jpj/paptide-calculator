Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions regarding peptide reconstitution math, concentration formulas, metric unit conversions, and safe educational usage.

A peptide calculator is an educational mathematical tool that helps users solve equations involving solute mass, liquid diluent volume, solution concentration, and volumetric draw amounts. It operates purely on mathematical formulas and the numbers entered by the user.

Concentration is calculated by dividing the total mass of the peptide by the total volume of liquid solvent added:
Concentration = Mass ÷ Volume
Both numbers are converted into base units (typically milligrams and milliliters) before calculation, yielding a concentration in mg/mL. Multiplying by 1,000 gives mcg/mL.

To convert milligrams (mg) to micrograms (mcg), multiply the number of milligrams by 1,000. For example: 2 mg × 1,000 = 2,000 mcg. Conversely, to convert micrograms to milligrams, divide by 1,000 (e.g., 250 mcg ÷ 1,000 = 0.25 mg).

To convert milliliters (mL) to liters (L), divide the milliliter amount by 1,000. For example: 500 mL ÷ 1,000 = 0.5 L. Conversely, to convert liters to milliliters, multiply by 1,000 (e.g., 0.002 L × 1,000 = 2 mL).

mg/mL represents milligrams of peptide mass dissolved per single milliliter of solution. It is the standard density metric used in laboratories to express liquid medication strength. A solution of 5 mg/mL contains 5 milligrams of active substance in each 1.0 mL of reconstituted liquid.

Required liquid volume is calculated by dividing the desired dose mass by the solution's concentration:
Volume (mL) = Desired Dose (mg) ÷ Concentration (mg/mL)
For example, if you want a dose of 0.5 mg from a 5 mg/mL concentration, the required volume is: 0.5 ÷ 5 = 0.1 mL.

A U-100 syringe has barrel tick markings where 100 units equal 1.0 mL of liquid. Each individual unit marking represents 0.01 mL (1/100th of a milliliter). To convert a volume in mL to U-100 units, simply multiply the milliliter volume by 100 (e.g. 0.15 mL × 100 = 15 units).
